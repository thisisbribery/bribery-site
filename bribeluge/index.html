BRIBELUGE

Bribeluge is an unofficial modified firmware for the Synthstrom Deluge, rebased on the latest official community firmware release (CFW v1.3.0) from delugecommunity.com, with thanks to everyone who has contributed to it.

I build Bribeluge for those who want more wonk, more destruction options, more speed, and a “studio in a box” mentality. I could not have done any of this without the incredible work of the original Deluge creators, Rohan Hill and Synthstrom Audible, and the tireless work of the community firmware team ever since the Deluge was open sourced.

A studio in a box

I built Bribeluge for myself, to chase the features and dream ideas I always wanted on the Deluge. The vision: the Deluge as a kind-of small studio of my favourite drum machines, synths and effects, all in one box. That cuts against the open canvas idea of hundreds of tracks and endless notes that the Deluge is sometimes thought of as. Bribeluge goes the other way: fewer tracks and fewer notes, with more quality and character in each one. So pick a handful of instruments, build the song around them, and treat it like a collection.

Read this before you flash. Bribeluge won't run as many voices or tracks at once as official community firmware 1.3. I'm working on stability and performance right now, but please don't test it by loading an old, dense song. You'll probably be disappointed. Instead, start a new song from the synth picker or drum picker and see if the studio approach sparks a different kind of creativity. You can flash back to CFW 1.3 at any time for the classic Deluge.

01

Drum machines

TRIBE · Cyc engines · LXR · GIN · ULTRAKIK · DXIA · DIIA · Autofill · Patterns

Synthesised drums on any kit row. A drum synth row is still a normal Deluge sound: its envelopes, filters, FX, mod matrix, automation and parameter locks all work on top. Every engine is in one list, SYNTH MODE, with Cycles-style gold knobs and one pad layout.

Every knob is centred on the real machine, with extras after Hexinverter's Mutant drums: a square wave and sub on the kick, a ringing snappy on the snare. Eleven kits built in.

Install

One zip holds the firmware and every preset. Four steps with the SD card.

  1. Download Bribeluge and unzip it. Inside are the firmware .bin, a PRESETS folder and these steps as INSTALL.txt.
  2. Firmware. Put the Deluge's SD card in your computer. Copy the .bin to the top level of the card, beside the KITS, SYNTHS and SONGS folders. Remove any other .bin files from the top level.
  3. Presets. Open KITS on the card and drag CYCLES and LXR into it from the zip's PRESETS/KITS. Then open SYNTHS on the card and drag BRINGS, BROPHET, BRUNO106, DX7 and WAVE into it from PRESETS/SYNTHS.
  4. Put the card back in the Deluge with it switched off. Hold SHIFT and switch it on. Let go once it starts loading the new firmware.

Worth knowing

  • Go one level in. Don't drag the zip's KITS or SYNTHS folder onto the card itself. On a Mac that replaces the card's whole folder, and your own kits and synths go with it.
  • Updating. Delete those seven preset folders from the card first, then copy the new ones: presets get retuned and renamed between versions. If you saved your own presets inside one, move them out first and put them back after.
  • WAVE presets play the free BVKER Custom Wavetables pack, which isn't mine to include. Unzip it into SAMPLES/WAVETABLES on the card. Everything else works without it.
  • Built in. The GIN and TRIBE kits and the ULTRAKIK kicks are in the firmware: nothing to copy.
  • Finding them. SHIFT + SYNTH for the synth browser, SHIFT + KIT for kit presets.
  • Just the firmware? The download window also has the .bin on its own. To go back, install an official firmware file the same way.

Status: experimental. Made on a 7-segment Deluge; the OLED screens haven't been checked on hardware. Back up your SD card before installing.

Why

The thinking behind it.

Almost a brand new ultra version of what the Deluge has been for me since about 2018.

A curated collection

Bribeluge treats the Deluge as a studio in a box: a collection of instruments you choose on purpose, each one deep enough to carry its part. It isn't aiming to be an infinite canvas with a million different tracks playing at once. Pick the tracks a song needs and give each one room. There is no track limit and no plan to add one: a song can still hold dozens of tracks. The emulated synths are heavier than the stock engines, so the more of them play at once, the sooner the CPU runs out.

Character first

Each engine trades raw voice count for sound and depth, so Bribeluge runs fewer voices at once than CFW 1.3. Stability and performance are what I'm working on now. It isn't built for everyone, and backwards compatibility isn't the priority. Older songs load, but big, dense ones are not where it shines.
